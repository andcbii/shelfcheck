# Separate provider catalogue freshness from Plex comparisons

Every full Plex scan checks the library inventory for changes. Retain downloaded TMDB and TVDB catalogues independently of comparison results so a coverage mapping or ownership change can trigger a new comparison without downloading otherwise reusable provider data. Saving, editing, or deleting Manual Matches, including bulk deletion, immediately rechecks only affected shows using saved catalogues and available saved library inventory. Ownership corrections follow this same policy. Include shows affected by both old and new members, and mark all affected shows for fresh provider downloads on the next user-run full library scan. Preserve unrelated comparisons and cached data; these mutations must not start or queue a full library scan. This replaces the earlier rule that mapping changes only mark comparisons pending until the next scan. Disable Manual Match and ownership-correction mutation controls during a scan and reject mapping saves, deletes, bulk deletes, and ownership corrections through the API while a scan is active, including requests from an already-open manager.

Persist each affected show's obligation to download fresh applicable TMDB and TVDB data separately from whether its cached comparison is up to date. This mandatory next-scan refresh overrides ordinary same-day reuse for affected shows. A cached recheck performs no Plex enumeration or provider catalogue downloads, does not consume the refresh obligation, and does not advance provider successful-download timestamps, the preceding-successful-full-scan baseline, or the daily successful-full-scan count. Refresh obligations survive restarts and failed downloads and clear independently per provider only after the required download succeeds. If saved comparison inputs are missing or incomplete, preserve available last-good results and expose a Coverage Issue rather than claiming an empty catalogue or starting a full scan.

Track each show's last successful catalogue download separately for TMDB and TVDB. Reusing data or rerunning a comparison does not reset that timestamp. Provider data older than 60 elapsed days must be refreshed during a scan. When the previous full Plex scan is over 14 elapsed days old, force a TMDB refresh; this gap alone does not force a TVDB refresh. Explicitly clearing a show's cache forces fresh provider downloads for that show on its next scan.

Continue provider change detection. For TMDB, a show flagged as changed may reuse a catalogue successfully downloaded on the same UTC date. This deliberately allows a later correction that day to wait until a subsequent day's scan. TVDB retains timestamp-based change checks independently. Persist successful full Plex scan counts per UTC day across restarts. On scans 5, 10, and so on, bypass TMDB's same-day reuse for library shows in that day's TMDB change list; do not force TVDB refreshes or refresh unchanged TMDB shows merely because the count was reached. Failed scan attempts do not advance the successful-scan count.

When a change check fails, retain available saved data, visibly mark freshness as unverified, and retry on the next sync. Shows without saved data still require downloads. Mandatory age-based refreshes take precedence over failed change checks and attempt the provider download anyway. If that download fails, retain the last good data, mark freshness as unverified, leave the successful-download timestamp unchanged, and retry on the next scan.

Record structured current Coverage Issues for comparison, inventory, provider verification, and download failures, including unverified catalogue freshness and failed refreshes for shows without Manual Matches. Retain provider/operation, readable reason, and known show/episode identities, with library-wide scope when a show cannot be identified. The Coverage Issues report and warning use this same issue state; resolving a cause clears the corresponding issue. Capture diagnostic context when failures occur rather than deriving it solely from a global pending flag.

This replaces library-wide provider fetching caused solely by the existence or revision of manual mappings. Independent provider catalogues require additional persisted data, but preserve the distinction between changes to provider descriptions and changes to local comparison inputs. No new per-show refresh action is planned.

## Delivery boundary

Issue #14 owns the shared backend: provider catalogue persistence and freshness, saved library/comparison inputs, one comparison engine used by full scans and local cached rechecks, affected-show invalidation, durable next-scan refresh obligations, active-scan mutation protection, and structured diagnostic state. Issue #15 depends on #14 and owns the Manual Matches and Coverage Issues reports, report navigation, Matching Episodes dialog, and their integration with those backend operations. It must not introduce a second comparator, cache policy, refresh queue, or diagnostic store. The accepted joint review replaces the original deferred-comparison wording in #14; both tickets now use immediate cached rechecks followed by mandatory affected-show provider downloads on the next user-run full scan.
